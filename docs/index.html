ROUTE TELEMETRY · 2021 NFL DATA

What Makes NFL Receivers Get Open?

We analyzed 5,470 route breaks to see how players use speed and changes of direction to create space from defenders.

Just like racing teams compare how two cars take the same turn, we compare how two NFL receivers run similar routes.

Types of route break are based on the angle of the turn (35–70°, 70–115°, 115°+), not on charted route names. The route names in brackets are what each type typically contains.

Bars show percentile among players at the same position with at least 100 routes. Turn numbers use only clean turns: at least 4.5 yd/s before the turn, at least 1 s after the snap, nearest defender 0.7–8 yd away.

Extra Space Created From the Defender

Which movement traits are most closely linked to a receiver getting open? Of the movement traits we measured, speed before the turn showed the clearest positive relationship with getting open.

Teal bar: linked to more space. Red bars: linked to less space. Grey: no clear link. Bars show extra space created from the defender for a one-step (one standard deviation) increase in each trait.
